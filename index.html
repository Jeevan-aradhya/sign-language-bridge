Settings

Hand shapes the app looks for. Show the palm to the camera, fingers up unless noted. J and Z are drawn in the air.

The built-in rules work for clear shapes, but every hand and camera is different. Record a few seconds of each letter and the app learns your hand. Samples stay in this browser unless you export them.

Tip for the project owner: export your samples, save the file as data/samples.json in the repository and push. Everyone who opens the site then gets your calibration by default.

  1. Capture. The browser camera streams video frames; nothing is uploaded.
  2. Hand detection. A MediaPipe hand model finds 21 landmarks per frame, in the image and in 3D.
  3. Features. Finger curl, thumb position, finger spread and hand direction are measured from the landmarks and scaled by palm size.
  4. Classification. Geometric rules pick the letter. If you calibrated, a nearest-neighbour model over your recorded samples takes over when it is confident.
  5. Stabilising. A vote over recent frames plus a hold timer turns noisy detections into deliberate key presses.
  6. Word building. Letters form words; an open hand adds a space; suggestions complete words from a dictionary.
  7. Output. The sentence is shown as text and read aloud with the browser's text-to-speech.

Based on the project seminar "Real-Time Sign Language Detection using Deep Convolutional Neural Networks" (KVG College of Engineering). The seminar model was a CNN over camera frames; this web version swaps in landmark features so it can run on any device without a server.

Recognises the 26 ASL fingerspelling letters (J and Z by motion) plus a space gesture. It is a learning and demonstration tool, not a replacement for a human interpreter.
